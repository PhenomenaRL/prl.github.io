Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Quantum State Control

Alice Johnson, Bob Wilson, and the PRL Team

Abstract: We explore the application of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) to the problem of maintaining coherence in simulated quantum environments. By treating individual qubits as agents within a decentralized actor-critic framework, we demonstrate improved stability and error correction in the presence of synthetic decoherence.

Introduction

Quantum state replication requires precise control over decoherence factors. Traditional control theory often struggles with the high-dimensional state space of multi-qubit systems, where the complexity grows exponentially with each added qubit. In this paper, we propose a scalable alternative using distributed AI.

Methodology

Our approach involves three key components:

  1. Decentralized Actor-Critic: Each qubit agent observes its local environment and partial state information, reducing the computational overhead of a global controller.
  2. Coherence Reward Function: Agents are incentivized to maintain state purity and minimize von Neumann entropy through a shared reward signal.
  3. Synthetic Noise Injection: We evaluate robustness by subjecting the system to simulated environmental interference modeled after actual hardware noise profiles.

Results

Our MARL approach achieves 25% longer coherence times compared to baseline PID controllers in simulated 8-qubit systems. Furthermore, the decentralized nature of the agents allows for near-linear scaling when increasing the number of qubits in the simulation.
