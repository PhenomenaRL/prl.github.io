Synthesizing Non-Newtonian Materials in Virtual Space

Dr. Sarah Chen, Michael Chang, and the PRL Team

Abstract: This research focuses on the high-fidelity replication of non-Newtonian fluid behaviors, such as shear-thickening and shear-thinning, within constrained digital environments. We introduce a particle-based optimization method that dynamically adjusts viscosity coefficients based on local stress tensors, allowing for the simulation of materials like oobleck and liquid armor with physical accuracy.

Introduction

Replicating materials that defy simple Newtonian physics is crucial for advanced protective equipment design and industrial processing simulations. Traditional grid-based solvers often fail to capture the abrupt phase transitions characteristic of these materials. We present a hybrid approach that combines Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) with a custom rheological constitutive model.

Methodology

Our approach involves three key components:

  1. Stress-Dependent Viscosity: We implemented a Power-law fluid model where the apparent viscosity is a function of the local shear rate, calculated per-particle.
  2. Boundary Interaction Layers: Custom collision handling that accounts for surface tension and adhesion properties unique to complex fluids.
  3. GPU-Accelerated Neighbor Search: To maintain real-time performance, we utilize a spatial hashing algorithm optimized for parallel execution on modern hardware.

Results

Our simulations successfully replicated the "hole-drilling" and "bouncing" effects observed in physical shear-thickening fluids. When compared to high-speed camera footage of real oobleck experiments, our virtual synthesis achieved a visual similarity index (VSI) of 0.94, a significant improvement over standard SPH implementations.
