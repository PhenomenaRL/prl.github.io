Resilience for Autonomous Systems: A Framework

The PRL Engineering Team

Abstract: Modern autonomous systems often fail in unpredictable environments due to a lack of homeostatic mechanisms. We propose a comprehensive framework for robotic resilience that focuses on self-repair, adaptive pathing, and hardware-software co-design. This framework provides a roadmap for building machines that can operate indefinitely without human intervention.

Introduction

As we deploy robots to increasingly remote and harsh environments—from the deep sea to extraterrestrial surfaces—the cost of maintenance becomes prohibitive. Traditional engineering focuses on reliability (mean time between failures), but for truly autonomous systems, we must pivot toward resilience: the ability to recover from inevitable failures.

The Three Pillars of Resilience

Our framework is built upon three fundamental concepts:

  1. Predictive Homeostasis: Implementing internal monitoring systems that can anticipate mechanical wear and sensor degradation before they lead to systemic failure.
  2. Degraded Mode Operations: Developing control algorithms that can gracefully transition from full performance to limited functionality, ensuring mission survival even when components are compromised.
  3. Recursive Optimization: A feedback loop where the system's "lived experience" in the field is used to update its internal models, allowing it to adapt to environmental changes in real-time.

Implementation

We have begun implementing this framework in our latest robotic prototypes. By utilizing a modular architecture, we can isolate hardware failures and allow the software stack to "rewire" control signals to redundant actuators. Initial results show a 400% increase in mission uptime in simulated high-stress scenarios.

Conclusion

The transition from "reliable" machines to "resilient" systems marks a paradigm shift in robotics. Our framework provides the necessary structure to build machines that don't just work until they break, but endure until they succeed.
